PART 1: CLINICAL ESCALATION & SEVERE SEPSIS MANAGEMENT (PROTOCOL SEP-3)
================================================================================

1.1 SCOPE AND TARGET POPULATION
This protocol applies to all adult inpatient units, the Emergency Department (ED), 
and the Intensive Care Unit (ICU). Pediatric patients (under 18 years) must be 
routed to Pediatric Protocol P-ED-44 immediately.

1.2 INITIAL SCREENING CRITERIA: SOFA SCORE THRESHOLDS
Staff must calculate the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score upon any 
patient presenting with two or more Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS) 
criteria:
- Body core temperature above 38.3°C or below 36.0°C.
- Heart rate greater than 90 beats per minute.
- Respiratory rate greater than 20 breaths per minute.
- White blood cell count (WBC) greater than 12,000/mcL or greater than 10% immature bands.

If a patient exhibits an acute increase of the SOFA score by 2 points or more from 
their known baseline, the bedside Registered Nurse (RN) must immediately trigger a 
"Code Sepsis" alert via the electronic health record (EHR) console.

1.3 TIME-CRITICAL INTERVENTIONS: THE 1-HOUR INITIATION BUNDLE
Upon triggering a Code Sepsis alert, the following five interventions must be 
initiated within exactly 60 minutes of zero-time (time of alert confirmation):
1. Serum Lactate Measurement: Draw venous or arterial blood for lactate testing. 
   If initial lactate is greater than 2.0 mmol/L, remeasure within 2 to 4 hours.
2. Blood Cultures: Obtain two sets of blood cultures (one aerobic, one anaerobic) 
   prior to antibiotic administration. However, antibiotic therapy must never be 
   delayed by more than 45 minutes if blood culture acquisition proves difficult.
3. Broad-Spectrum Antimicrobial Administration: Administer IV Piperacillin-Tazobactam 
   (3.375g every 6 hours via extended 4-hour infusion). In patients with documented 
   penicillin anaphylaxis, substitute with Vancomycin (15-20 mg/kg IV loading dose) 
   combined with Cefepime (2g IV every 8 hours).
4. Rapid Fluid Resuscitation: If mean arterial pressure (MAP) is below 65 mmHg or 
   initial blood lactate is 4.0 mmol/L or greater, begin rapid IV administration of 
   crystalloid solution (specifically Plasma-Lyte A or Lactated Ringer's) at 
   30 mL per kilogram of ideal body weight. Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) is explicitly 
   prohibited for volumes exceeding 1,000 mL due to hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis risks.
5. Vasopressor Administration: If MAP remains below 65 mmHg following 50% completion 
   of the crystalloid bolus, immediately initiate peripheral Norepinephrine at 
   0.05 mcg/kg/min, titrating upward by 0.02 mcg/kg/min every 5 minutes to achieve 
   a target MAP between 65 and 75 mmHg.

1.4 REPORTING HIERARCHY AND CLINICAL OVERSIGHT
The bedside RN reports immediately to the Medical Officer on Duty (MOOD). If MAP 
does not respond within 30 minutes of initiating vasopressors, the MOOD must escalate 
directly to the Critical Care Specialist (Intensivist On Call). The Intensivist on Call 
assumes primary command of the case and retains sole authority to order invasive 
arterial line placement or secondary inotropic agents (Epinephrine or Vasopressin).

PART 2: PHARMACEUTICAL CONTRAINDICATIONS & MEDICATION RECONCILIATION
================================================================================

2.1 HIGH-ALERT MEDICATION PROTOCOLS
All prescription orders for therapeutic anticoagulants, antiarrhythmic agents, and 
controlled analgesics must be verified through dual-pharmacist independent review 
prior to automated dispensing cabinet (ADC) release.

2.2 NOVEL ORAL ANTICOAGULANT (NOAC) & DIRECT ORAL ANTICOAGULANT (DOAC) RESTRICTIONS
Apixaban (Eliquis) and Rivaroxaban (Xarelto) are classified as Class II High-Alert 
Anticoagulants. 
- In patients undergoing emergent surgery, Apixaban must be suspended for a minimum 
  of 48 hours prior to incision for high-bleed-risk procedures, or 24 hours for 
  low-bleed-risk interventions.
- Reversal Agent Protocols: In cases of life-threatening hemorrhagic shock secondary 
  to Factor Xa inhibitors (Apixaban or Rivaroxaban), the attending emergency physician 
  must administer Andexanet Alfa (Andexxa). 
  - Low-dose regimen: 400 mg IV bolus followed by 4 mg/min for 120 minutes.
  - High-dose regimen: 800 mg IV bolus followed by 8 mg/min for 120 minutes (mandatory 
    if the last dose of Factor Xa inhibitor was taken within 8 hours).
- If Andexanet Alfa is unavailable or out of stock, 4-Factor Prothrombin Complex 
  Concentrate (4F-PCC / Kcentra) is approved as a secondary alternative at a fixed 
  dose of 50 units/kg (maximum 5,000 units), subject to immediate verbal approval from 
  the Chief of Transfusion Medicine.

2.3 CRITICAL PHARMACOKINETIC DRUG-DRUG INTERACTIONS
- Clarithromycin, Ketoconazole, and Ritonavir are potent CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein (P-gp) 
  inhibitors. Co-administration with Apixaban is contraindicated; dose reduction by 
  50% is permitted only if no clinical alternative exists and serum anti-Xa levels 
  can be monitored every 12 hours.
- Amiodarone and Warfarin: When initiating Amiodarone in a patient stabilized on Warfarin, 
  the weekly Warfarin dosage must automatically be decreased by 33% to 50% starting on Day 1 
  to prevent profound INR elevation. INR must be re-checked on Days 3, 7, and 14.

2.4 INCIDENT DOCUMENTATION & ADVERSE DRUG REACTION (ADR) REPORTING
Any adverse drug reaction categorized as Severity Grade 3 (severe, non-life-threatening) 
or Grade 4 (life-threatening) must be submitted via the Internal Safety Event Portal (ISEP) 
within 4 hours of stabilization. The Clinical Pharmacist Specialist must submit a 
formal MedWatch Form FDA 3500 within 24 hours to the Patient Safety Committee.

PART 3: COMPLIANCE, INCIDENT INVESTIGATION, AND AUDIT GOVERNANCE
================================================================================

3.1 REGULATORY COMPLIANCE OVERSIGHT
All clinical departments operate under the joint oversight of the Department of Clinical 
Governance, the Joint Commission Standards Office, and the Hospital Quality Assurance 
Committee (HQAC). 

3.2 ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS (RCA) WORKFLOW FOR SENTINEL EVENTS
A Sentinel Event is defined as an unexpected occurrence involving death, serious physical 
or psychological injury, or the immediate risk thereof. Sentinel events include, but are 
not limited to: medication administration errors resulting in cardiac arrest, wrong-site 
surgical incisions, inpatient suicides, and unmonitored falls resulting in intracranial hemorrhage.

When a Sentinel Event occurs, the following strict timeline must be executed:
- Within 2 Hours: The Unit Nurse Manager must verbally notify the Chief Medical Officer 
  (CMO) and the Chief Quality Officer (CQO).
- Within 12 Hours: The Patient Safety Liaison must freeze all medical records and audit logs 
  associated with the event. No retroactive chart modifications or late progress notes are 
  permitted without explicit digital sign-off from the Legal Risk Management Department.
- Within 72 Hours: An interdisciplinary Root Cause Analysis (RCA) Team is formed. The RCA 
  Team consists of:
  * The Lead Patient Safety Investigator (Team Lead)
  * The Chief Pharmacist (or designated Clinical Specialist)
  * The Nursing Clinical Practice Director
  * Two independent, non-involved peer physicians from external clinical units.
- Within 30 Calendar Days: The RCA Team must submit a complete Comprehensive Systematic 
  Analysis and Action Plan (CSAAP) to the HQAC Board of Directors.

3.3 CLINICAL AUDIT THRESHOLDS AND CORRECTIVE ACTION PLANS (CAP)
Every quarterly reporting period, clinical units undergo random audits assessing 15% of all 
discharged patient charts.
- Standard Compliance Benchmark: Units must achieve a minimum overall compliance score of 88%.
- Conditional Probation: If a unit achieves between 75% and 87.9%, the Department Chairperson 
  must submit a 60-day Corrective Action Plan (CAP) to the Chief Quality Officer.
- Mandatory Suspension of Elective Services: If a unit scores below 75% for two consecutive 
  quarters, the CMO retains executive authority to suspend elective admissions or non-emergent 
  procedures in that specific clinical department until an external audit validates full remediation.

3.4 WHISTLEBLOWER PROTECTION AND ETHICS HOTLINE
Hospital employees, medical residents, and contract staff who report safety violations, 
unauthorized protocol alterations, or falsification of audit data are protected under Network 
Policy GOV-W-104 (Whistleblower Integrity Act). Retaliatory measures—including schedule 
demotions, unfavorable performance evaluations, or administrative leave—are strictly illegal. 
Suspected non-compliance may be reported 24/7 anonymously via the Compliance Ethics Hotline 
(Extension 4490) or directly to the Director of Regulatory Integrity.
